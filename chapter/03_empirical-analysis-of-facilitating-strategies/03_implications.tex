This section records how the results are carried into the design.
It adds no further evidence.
The identifiers are the rows of \autoref{tab:interview-ai-functions} and
\autoref{tab:interview-ai-limits}.
They are separate from the codebook, which uses some of the same letters for facilitation
practice.
The thesis is scoped to a proactive intervention while a convergent meeting is still running.
Functions before or after that conversation, and functions that current tools already
approximate, stay outside the system.

F7 and F8 are that before and after.
Preparation and a written handoff are conditions of a sound decision, and the interviews
treat them as at least as important as live moderation.
An in-meeting system does not own them.
Announcing a goal that is already written down would be a fixed text, read aloud by
text-to-speech.
That announcement does not use the running conversation, so it is set aside with F8.
Noticing later that the group is deciding without the goal, or against it, does use the
conversation, and it stays in as F4.

F6, a persona for an absent stakeholder or a missing expertise, is a different layer.
Where a role is missing, the facilitators' move is to ask the group what that role would say
and to mark the answer as a hypothesis.
Simulating the person lies outside this thesis.

F1, a fast summary or a visual restatement, repeats a move the group already has.
Cards and boards make the state visible in the room, and current meeting platforms already
transcribe, keep a timeline, and post reminders in the chat.
The contribution sought here is the process intervention those tools do not take from
facilitation practice.
Making the state visible remains a human move.

The functions that remain are taken forward only inside that practice and inside the limits.
The meeting opens before it closes.
Clarification stays separate from debate, and the groan zone is endured.
Time pressure is what closes the search too early, so an intervention that hurries the group
from the first options to a choice repeats that failure.
Bringing a person in is selective: the relevant voice is the one whose later resistance would
matter.

L3 forbids treating every silence as a gap, because a prompt can shame a quiet person or
freeze someone who was about to change their mind.
Integrating a participant therefore means a question that makes a missing stake or a missing
fact sayable.
L5 keeps the decision with the group.
The system must not decide, and it must not impose a structure nobody asked for.
No single cue justifies the same intervention every time.
L4 and L7 settle the floor.
People will not yield it to a machine, so interruption is allowed only by prior agreement,
as a mode the group switches on or as a signal it can choose to follow.
L6, L1, and L2 settle what may be read.
A transcript is not enough context, sentiment and attendance monitoring become surveillance,
and a record chills dissent.
The words can support a pointer at an unnamed decision, a thin agreement, a skipped step, a
named bias, or a missing voice or fact.
A score for mood or presence is outside that.
A missing or false decision scope, process and rights sits under the meeting.
Where it is absent, the honest act is to say so.

F2 is the process condition taken from that material.
Timekeeping is an agreed interruption when the clock is closing the search, distinct from a
scheduled chat reminder.
It makes the judgment urgent: whether to go back, to stay in the middle, or to agree only
the path to a decision.
That judgment stays with the group.
F3, F4, and F5 are the focus.
F3 proposes the next process step the talk has skipped: the question, the options, doing
nothing, pros and cons, or a named procedure.
F4 points, as a check, at a decision the group has not named, or at agreement that is thinner
than it appears.
F5 cues a named bias by asking for the voice or the fact that bias would leave out.
The follow-up question and the integration of a participant belong to that cue, and both stay
questions.
The requirements in \autoref{sec:requirements-analysis} are taken from this cut: which of
these acts can be performed from the running conversation, and which of the restraints above
are conditions of a valid intervention.
